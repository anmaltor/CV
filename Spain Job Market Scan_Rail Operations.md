# Spain job-market scan — rail & infrastructure roles

_Personal working note for Antonio Mallol Torralbo. Prepared 2026-07-16. Built from `CV Antonio Mallol_Dec2025_FLOW_.pdf`. Not a CTSM work product; not for distribution._

## The fit, in one paragraph

The profile is rare in the Spanish market: a Spanish national, Valencia-trained
industrial engineer with an IESE Executive MBA, who has run **every phase of a
rail PPP lifecycle** — bid and ConOps design (Bechtel), asset handover and
systems integration (Boxfish / OnCorr), and live steady-state operations at
COO / General Manager level (CTSM, Eglinton Crosstown) — plus eight years as
Maintenance Director on a live Spanish concession (Metro Ligero Oeste, Madrid,
2006–2015, 99.5% availability). Very few candidates can credibly sit across
bid, construction handover, and operations at once. That combination is the
strongest card to play against Spanish infrastructure groups that are
expanding their O&M and concessions businesses internationally.

## What's live right now (verifiable signals, 2026-07-16)

A direct pass over public job boards surfaced mostly technician-grade postings.
Senior O&M and COO-level seats in this sector are rarely advertised openly in
Spain — they move through retained search and internal networks. Two concrete
signals from this scan:

- **Ineco** — open requisitions at director grade for rail: _Director/a de
  Proyectos Ferroviarios_ (ref. 25886) and _Director/a de Obra Ferroviaria de
  Infraestructura y Vía_ (ref. TRE26-EEM-031), Madrid. Both are
  civil-engineering-credentialed, so not a direct match on paper — but they
  confirm Ineco is hiring at director level for rail. Worth a conversation
  about the O&M / international-assignment side of the house rather than
  applying to these specific reqs.
- **Renfe** — a 360-post maintenance hiring wave announced for its Ingeniería y
  Mantenimiento division (application window closed April 2026). Not an
  executive signal directly, but it indicates Renfe is scaling its maintenance
  organisation — often a leading indicator that O&M leadership seats follow
  within 6–18 months.

Treat this as a snapshot, not a shortlist — assume it is stale within weeks
given how this segment hires.

## Why the timing may work

- **HSR liberalisation is still maturing.** Iryo and Ouigo España are past
  launch but still building full lifecycle operating capability — a seat where
  "I've delivered construction handover through steady-state" is a
  differentiator, not a checkbox.
- **Spanish groups are exporting O&M, not just construction.** Acciona, ACS,
  and Comsa increasingly compete on decades-long concession operating contracts
  abroad, and need Madrid/Barcelona-based executives who can run those
  contracts globally — exactly the CTSM / Boxfish / Bechtel arc.
- **Renfe and Ineco are both scaling internationally** (Texas, Haramain,
  Riyadh, Panama, Lima) and need people who have mobilised an O&M organisation
  from scratch, not just designed one on paper.

## How to actually surface these roles

At this seniority, public boards under-represent what's available. Higher-yield
channels:

- **Retained executive search with an infrastructure/industrial practice** —
  the Spain offices of Egon Zehnder, Spencer Stuart, Odgers Berndtson, Heidrick
  & Struggles, and Russell Reynolds run infrastructure and transport mandates;
  Page Executive and Hays Executive cover the tier just below pure C-suite.
  Personalised direct outreach beats waiting for a posted role.
- **IESE alumni network** — the EMBA cohort and IESE's infrastructure / PPP
  faculty are a legitimate, underused route into Acciona, ACS, Ferrovial, and
  Globalvia leadership.
- **Warm outreach to the Metro Ligero Oeste parent consortium** (Acciona /
  Comsa / Alstom lineage) — a known quantity there beats a cold application
  anywhere else.
- **LinkedIn "open to work", recruiters-only**, with alerts on "Director
  Operaciones", "Director Mantenimiento", "Head of Rail", and "COO" filtered to
  Spain — cheap to set up, catches the minority of roles that do get posted.
